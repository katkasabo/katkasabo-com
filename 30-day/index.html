Day 04 Saturday, 5 September 2026 # 518 words

On Friday's night with play R.O.I. by the art critic I never became

A part of me has always dreamed of being an art critic. Book reviewer. Pop culture commentator.

It feels like a great role - you can talk and imbibe yourself with all this fun stuff WHILE not having to have your own skin in the game.

The perks of keeping yourself at the sidelines.

I remember having read about Oprah's dad making her read books and write reviews about them and that sounded like helluva great upbringing.

So this was the intro into telling you about the play I saw last night.

R.O.I.

A promo poster caught my attention while shopping in Inner Sunset's Luke's - I do have a soft spot for live performances and I do talk a lot about ROI in my daily work.

Done - tickets bought. I had never heard of the SF Playhouse, a non profit theater production with one of their venues at Yerba Buena. The neighboring Children's Creativity Museum seemed also like a worthwhile place to visit if you have little kids.

Play itself.

As I was getting settled in my seat, I was well aware of how tired, beaten up I felt after the long week. Cautiously wiggling in my seat to see if it was comfortable enough to sneak in a little snooze if the story on the stage was not unfolding fast enough.

I spent the next 90 minutes propped up at the edge of that seat.

See, I did not become an art critic, so I cannot dissect expertly why I think the play was so good.

The story evolved about a young venture capitalist who's hustling hard to find the next big thing that will make her and her experienced boss - who backed Steve Jobs back in the day - crazy amounts of money.

Socially awkward yet determined biomolecular PhD busts in and despite the lack of polish, she manages to convince the VC to write her the first check.

Medical innovation that will change the world. No wonder the promo poster styled the main character after Theranos's Holmes.

Breakthrough innovation follows, company grows and everybody's getting giddy excited with the prospects of the greenbacks payday.

If the general story arc was guessable sight unseen, the details, the trials and tribulations the characters had to go through was painting their lives, stakes and dilemmas in painstaking details. It never felt fabricated by someone who chatted about venture capital with Claude. I was not surprised to learn after the play that its author, Aaron Loeb, used to work as a venture capitalist himself.

Then the play was over and the imaginary curtain fell - venue had no actual stage, it felt more like an intimate, indoor, a little crammed amphitheater - I was left to digest characters' choices by myself walking back to my N train stop.

I learned from the playbill that SF Playhouse sees itself as the Empathy Gym, "making the edgy accessible and the accessible edgy". Last night it delivered on giving the audience an emotional peek into the worlds of people driving & deciding about cutting-edge innovation.

Standing ovation & BRAVO.

Day 01 Wednesday, 2 September 2026 # 108 words

Please take some lemons

Welcome to my first day of Everything Is Fair Game blogging.

I love what this lady did. She's sitting quietly at co-work but her desk says "Please Take Some Lemons, Basils, Thyme...".

This is breaking the pattern. Why is someone just offering something like this? It opens the curiosity loop. Unexplained generosity. So much better than "come talk to me about XYZ".

How can I swipe this and use it myself? When I want people to do certain action, instead of straight up asking for it, how can I set up their curiosity in a way that leads to them taking the action to close their curiosity loop.

Later 3 September 66 words

So apparently her name is Shirley, she works at Rivian (the co-work I was in is located inside the Rivian dealership), and what she brought is the surplus produce from her own backyard. I couldn't resist and chatted her up before I had to leave. There were 2 other people talking to her before that. Clearly I was not the only one curiosity-looped. This $hit works!
